Executive Summary SafeChat AI is a web-based artificial intelligence application designed to detect toxic, harmful, and unsafe content in chat conversations. The system targets parents, educators, organizations, and individuals who need to monitor digital communication for safety threats such as cyberbullying, harassment, hate speech, and inappropriate messaging. The platform provides two core operational modes: offline analysis of exported chat files (such as WhatsApp exports) and real-time live monitoring of WhatsApp conversations via QR-code authentication. Results are visualized through an interactive dashboard with detailed safety reports, risk scores, and labeled message classifications. The project is developed by a three-member team with clearly divided responsibilities spanning frontend development, AI/ML integration, and real-time messaging infrastructure — all delivered within a focused two-day sprint. 2. Problem Statement Digital communication platforms such as WhatsApp, Telegram, and social media messengers are widely used by children, teenagers, and adults. However, the open and largely unmoderated nature of these platforms creates significant risks: Cyberbullying and harassment are increasingly prevalent among school-age users. Hate speech, explicit content, and threats can circulate unchecked in private or group chats. Parents and guardians lack accessible tools to monitor their children&apos;s digital safety without invasive surveillance. Organizations have limited means to audit internal communication for policy violations or toxic behavior. Existing moderation solutions are either too expensive, too technical, or not designed for private messaging contexts. SafeChat AI addresses this gap by offering an easy-to-use, AI-powered safety scanner that works directly with WhatsApp data — the most widely used messaging platform globally. 3. System Architecture SafeChat AI follows a three-tier architecture comprising a React-based frontend, a Python backend (Flask/FastAPI), and an SQLite database. The live monitoring component runs as a separate Node.js microservice that interfaces with WhatsApp Web via the whatsapp-web.js library and communicates with the backend over HTTP and WebSockets.
